# The target frame

`qa/reference/warframe-target.jpg` is a real Warframe gameplay screenshot. The
player handed it over and said, in as many words: get the picture to this
quality. It is no longer an abstract "AAA" bar. It is this frame.

Open it. Look at it before you touch anything. Everything below is a reading
of what is actually in it, written so you can check your own frame against it
point by point.

## What the frame is

A third-person gameplay capture, not a posed marketing shot. The HUD is on,
the character is mid-traversal on industrial decking, and the composition was
not arranged for the camera. That matters: our frames have to survive the same
scrutiny as an ordinary moment of play, not just a hero angle.

## Camera

- **Close and over the shoulder.** The character occupies roughly the lower
  left third and is about 45% of the frame height. Ours sits centred, smaller,
  and further back, which reads as a tech demo rather than a game.
- **Off-axis and slightly rolled.** The horizon is not level and the character
  is not centred. The frame has a diagonal running through it.
- **Foreground framing.** A railing and a machine mass sit between the lens and
  the character at the left edge, out of focus and dark. That near layer is
  what makes the image feel like a space rather than a backdrop.

## Environment — the biggest gap

- **Greeble density.** Every surface carries small hard-surface detail:
  conduit runs, bolted flanges, vents, grating, cable trays, pressure
  cylinders, handrails, riveted plates. There is no square metre of flat
  anything. Count the distinct small objects in a 200 px square of that image
  and then count them in ours.
- **Asymmetry.** Nothing tiles. Pipes bend at different angles, panels are
  different sizes, machinery clusters are irregular. Our walls repeat a module
  on a grid, and the panel called that out twice.
- **Layered depth.** Foreground machine, mid-ground deck and character,
  background structure, distant haze. Four planes, each separated by value.
- **Organic intrusion.** Green foliage grows over the metal at the top and
  right. A second material family breaking up the hard surface is a large part
  of why the image does not read as a CAD render. We have nothing equivalent —
  no dust sheets, no hanging cable, no cloth, no growth, no debris.

## Materials

- **Everything is dirty.** Rust streaks running down from fixings, grime
  pooling in recesses, edge wear where paint has gone, dust settled on upward
  faces. No surface is its authored colour.
- **Roughness varies across a single surface.** Wet-looking patches next to
  matte dusty ones. That variation is what makes the specular break up instead
  of sheeting.
- **Ambient occlusion is everywhere.** Every joint, every recess, every place
  two objects meet has a dark contact gradient. This is the single most
  reliable tell of a rendered-well image versus a lit-flat one.

## Character

- **Dark, not bright.** The frame is a black-brown rubber and dark metal, with
  small gold and glowing accents. Ours is a pale ivory figure, which makes it
  read as plastic and flattens it against the architecture.
- **Wet specular.** Sharp, small, moving highlights along every edge and curve.
  The form is described by the highlight, not by the albedo.
- **Slim and long.** Long lower legs, a narrow waist, a small head. It
  occupies a tall thin column of the frame.
- **Held weapon, visible barrel.** The rifle is in both hands, angled across
  the body, with the muzzle reading clearly.

## Colour and grade

- **Warm and desaturated.** Ochre, rust brown, warm grey, with green as the
  only saturated note. No blue cast anywhere.
- **Bright ambient, soft shadows.** This is a daylit scene with a lot of
  bounce. Shadows are soft and deep but not black, and the highlight end is
  controlled — nothing clips to paper white, including the brightest sky
  showing through the structure.
- **Atmospheric perspective.** The background loses contrast and warms toward
  the haze. Distance is carried by value and contrast, not by fog colour.

## HUD

Far lighter than ours. A small minimap top left, one line of objective text
under it, health and shield as plain numerals top right, weapon and ammo
bottom right. Thin type, no heavy panels, no large chips, nothing with a
filled background. Our HUD occupies several times the area and reads as a web
dashboard laid over the render by comparison.

## How to use this

When you finish a change, capture the equivalent frame and put it beside this
one. Ask the specific question your axis owns:

- environment: does a 200 px square of my wall have as many distinct objects?
- materials: is any surface still its authored colour with no dirt?
- light: does every joint have a contact gradient?
- character: is the form described by highlights, and is it dark?
- post: is anything clipping to white, and is there a blue cast?
- HUD: how much of the frame area is covered, against roughly 8% there?
